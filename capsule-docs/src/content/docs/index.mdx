## What Capsule is

<CardGrid stagger>
	<Card title="End-to-end encrypted" icon="approve-check">
		The server stores opaque ciphertext and never holds a key. It cannot read an
		album title, a caption, or a photo — which is also why search and organization
		run on your device rather than on it.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Self-hosted" icon="laptop">
		Your server, your disks. Federation between servers is designed so sharing does
		not require anyone to centralize.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Recoverable by construction" icon="seti:db">
		Every asset is paired with a signed metadata sidecar, so a library is readable
		without a database and reconstructible from partial corruption.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Built for originals" icon="seti:image">
		RAW and video are first-class. Nothing is silently recompressed, and derivatives
		are signed so a corrupted thumbnail cannot pass for a good one.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## Why it is built this way

Most photo services ask you to choose between the features that come from a server
reading your library and the privacy that comes from it not being able to. Capsule
takes the second and rebuilds the features on the client: tagging, search, and
grouping run against a local index over data only your devices can decrypt.

That choice has costs, and the design docs state them rather than hiding them — what
a key-free server [can and cannot tell you](/design/notifications/), what happens
[when a key is lost](/design/cryptography/failure-modes/), and what a faulty or
hostile client [is prevented from doing](/design/threat-model/).
